## 2. Content pillars (formats) and how they perform

Based on the 30 most recent uploads:

### A. "What on Earth is X?" — the flagship format
Deep-dives on **oddly-classified places**: microstates, crown dependencies, unrecognised countries, remote territories.
- *What on Earth is the Isle of Man? (Not UK)* — **3.1M views** (channel's biggest recent hit)
- *What on Earth are the Sandwich Islands?* — 357K
- *What on Earth is Andorra? (European Microstate)* — 85K
- *What On Earth Is Bougainville?* — 50K
- *What on Earth is Kosovo? (Partially Recognised Country)* — 13K

The winning ingredient is a **status paradox**: "it's not a country, not part of the UK, and not a territory — so what IS it?"

### B. "What's Life Like in X?" — lived-experience angle
- *What Is Life Like In The Outback? (Australia)* — **2.1M views**
- *What's Life Like in Tasmania, Australia?* — 456K
- *What Life Is REALLY Like in the World's Richest City* — 13K

### C. "Why X" — explainer angle
- *Why Guam Even Exists* — 353K
- *Why Is Alpine Europe So Rich?* — 46K

### D. Listicles / fact compilations
- *Investigating 13 Bizarre Islands* — 974K
- *30 Geography Facts That Make No Sense* — 672K

### E. "What If" hypotheticals (weakest pillar)
- *What If We Drained The Mediterranean Sea?* — 24K
- *What If Every Country Had Three Capitals?* — 26K

### F. Long-form compilations (recent experiment)
Re-packaged existing segments as 52m–2.5h "relax / fall asleep" videos. Modest views (11K–24K) but likely strong watch-time.

**Takeaway:** the channel's outliers (1M+) are almost all *quirky territory deep-dives* (A) and *life-in-a-remote-place* (B). The safest bet for a new video is format A applied to a territory with a strong status paradox.

## 3. Hook analysis (from transcripts)

Both studied videos open with the exact same 4-beat cold open, delivered in ~25 seconds over a map zoom:

1. **Mystery location tease** — start on the map, not the name:
   > "Found in the Irish Sea in between Great Britain and Ireland, you will find this very interesting island…"
   > "Found quite literally in the middle of absolute nowhere in the vast Pacific Ocean lies an island of incredible strategic importance…"
2. **Status paradox** — stack the contradictions:
   > "It is actually not part of the UK, nor is it a country or territory — so what on Earth is it?"
3. **Curiosity stack** — 2–3 open questions promising the video's segments:
   > "What goes on there? And why does it have one of the weirdest flags on Earth?"
4. **Signature call-to-adventure** — a recurring catchphrase:
   > "So let's swim across and investigate."

## 4. The repeatable episode structure ("scenario")

Every deep-dive follows the same segment order. This is effectively their show bible:

| # | Segment | Devices used |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cold-open hook (map first, name ~20s in) | Mystery tease, paradox, question stack, "let's swim across" |
| 2 | Geography & location | Distances to neighbours, "if it were a country it would rank…" |
| 3 | Political status explained | Quote the official jargon, then: *"but what on Earth does that gibberish actually mean?"* → plain-English translation |
| 4 | Population & density | Relatable comparisons (fits in Wembley Stadium; same density as China) |
| 5 | Language / culture quirk | Play audio of endangered language, personal reaction |
| 6 | Economy, tax, currency | GDP compared to a small country; honest caveat: *"take this figure with a pinch of salt"* |
| 7 | Terrain, climate, "vibe" | Ice-age origin story, records ("highest temperature ever…") |
| 8 | The flag | Saved for late in the video despite being teased in the hook |
| 9 | The famous thing | "It's impossible to make a video about X and not mention…" (TT race, military bases) |
| 10 | Safety / quality of life | Crowdsourced rankings with a scepticism disclaimer |
| 11 | Outro | "So there we have it, X in a nutshell." → engagement question ("Would you ever visit? Let us know in the comments") → like & subscribe → "as always, thank you very much for watching" |

### Recurring stylistic devices
- **Rhetorical questions as transitions** — nearly every segment opens with one ("But what currency do they actually use?").
- **Relatable scale comparisons** — Wembley Stadium, Rhode Island, Montenegro's economy, China's density.
- **"If it were a country/state it would rank…"** — used at least twice per video.
- **Self-aware honesty** — "this is clutching at straws a little, to be honest", "use your own judgment with this one". Builds trust and reads as charmingly amateur.
- **First-person British persona** — "being from England myself…", personal reactions to language clips.
- **Title formulas** — "What on Earth is X? (Parenthetical Status Correction)", "Why X Even Exists", "What's Life Like in X?". The parenthetical ("Not UK") does heavy lifting: it front-loads the paradox in search results.

## 5. Topic selection for the new script

Criteria derived from their outliers: (1) a territory most viewers have heard of *or* can instantly locate, (2) a genuinely confusing legal status, (3) at least one jaw-dropper fact, (4) a weird flag or map artefact, (5) not already covered by the channel.

**Chosen topic: Saint-Pierre and Miquelon** — a piece of France 25 km off the coast of Canada. It scores on every axis: EU currency in North America, the "baguette" maritime corridor, Al Capone and Prohibition smuggling, the only guillotine ever used in North America, and its own 30-minutes-off time zone. The channel has covered nearby Greenland and many islands but never this one.
